## The question I get asked most

When companies overseas ask me about running an event in Tokyo, the first question is almost always the same: "Roughly how much will it cost in total?" It is a fair question, and it deserves an honest answer.

## The honest answer: only a small part can be put in a table

Here is the honest answer. The only costs I can show you in a table are the venue fees that some venues publish, and translation or interpretation rates. Everything else depends on what you actually do.

Two "event operations" quotes can look nothing alike, because the price moves with the level of finish you want, the number of attendees, what the event is for, and how many staff we put on it. That is why this column does not give you a "typical cost by event size" chart. If I made one, it would not match your event.

Two things to keep in mind. First, this is the exception rather than the rule: many Tokyo venues do not publish fees at all, and you get a number only by asking. Second, these figures are the room only. Audio-visual equipment, catering, staff, and operations come on top.

And the part that is hardest to predict is the small items. Take something as ordinary as a chair. On a quote, a chair is a few hundred yen per day. That looks like nothing — until you need a thousand of them for two days, and the same logic applies to tables, signage, cables, and every other line. None of it is expensive on its own. Together, it can quietly become one of the biggest lines in the budget.

## What to tell us first, so you get a useful quote

When someone asks "how much?", three things make it much easier for me to give a real answer. Rough is fine.

1. **What kind of event, and what it is for.** Lead generation? Branding? Something else?
2. **Your budget ceiling.** The line you will not go past.
3. **Your timeline.** When it needs to happen.

These three change the price completely. Rushed, large, and maximum exposure will be expensive. Starting small can be done for far less than most people expect.

## Three things that matter more than the price list

**1. Put one person on your team who speaks English and Japanese and understands events.**
That one person changes how fast things move and how many vendors you can actually choose from. I wrote about who that person is and what they actually do in [an earlier column](https://www.conec-tando.com/en/column/running-an-event-in-japan-without-japanese/).

**2. Get quotes from Japanese-only companies too.**
There are a fair number of companies in Tokyo that can work in English. But they usually cost more than companies that work only in Japanese. Translation tools are good enough now. Ask for a quote from a company with no English on its website — with a translation tool if you need one.

**3. Do not decide on price alone.**
How fast they reply, and whether you can work with them, matter just as much. As a rule, cheap turns out to be expensive.

## Talk to me first — and to one more company

Prices really are all over the map. I do not think that is unique to Tokyo; it is the same anywhere.

So talk to me first. Nothing needs to be decided yet — a rough, casual conversation is fine.

But do not stop at me. Get at least one more quote from another company. Comparing before you decide is better for both of us.

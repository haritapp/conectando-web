Organizers from overseas often ask me, "We want to run an event in Tokyo. Where should we rent a venue?" When I hear that, I don't start with venues. There are a few things that need to be settled first, or everything gets redone later. This article walks through the questions I actually ask, in the order I ask them, until a venue is decided.

## What I ask first

The first things I ask for are the timing, the number of attendees, the purpose, and, if possible, the budget. It also helps enormously if you can share a link to an event you have already run overseas: a website or a recap. It shows the atmosphere, the kind of people who attend, and the scale, all at a glance.

## Timing: does it clash with a major event in your industry?

This is not specific to Tokyo, but if your dates fully overlap with a major event in your industry, people won't come. For startups, that means events like [IVS](https://www.ivs.events/) (held in Kyoto) and [SusHi Tech Tokyo](https://sushitech-startup.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/). If you are in a different industry, overlapping is not a problem. The reverse can also work: scheduling close to those dates on purpose, to catch people who are already in Japan.

## Attendees: do you really need that many?

Many organizers tell me, "We get 1,000 people back home." It is worth pausing to ask whether the first edition in Tokyo needs the same number. Your event back home probably didn't start that big either. If the venue looks empty at the first edition, the event becomes hard to continue. I have seen many events that ended after one edition.

## Budget: what is left after the venue?

I ask about the budget early to avoid one specific situation: the venue eating the entire budget. The size and type of venue should be decided after you see what remains once the venue is paid for. I wrote about the cost breakdown in [How Much Does an Event in Tokyo Cost?](https://www.conec-tando.com/en/column/how-much-does-an-event-in-tokyo-cost/).

## Area: your industry largely decides the location

The area is largely determined by the industry. For startups, for example, Shibuya and Roppongi are common. At the same time, areas that want to attract your industry sometimes offer venues at lower rates. Toranomon is one example.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/toranomon_hills_forum/

## Public facilities: sometimes free or low-cost

Some public facilities, including those run by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, can be used free of charge or at low cost. Tokyo Innovation Base (TIB) is one example. Availability is limited and there are conditions for use, so if it could work for you, it is worth checking early.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/tokyo-innovation-base/

## Booking: popular venues fill up a year ahead

Popular venues can be booked out a year in advance. So rather than narrowing down to one venue, I start by contacting several and collecting candidates and their availability.

## Only then do we talk about venue type

Timing, attendees, purpose, budget, area, and booking outlook. Once these are settled, we can finally talk about whether it should be a hotel, a rental hall, or a convention center. I covered the differences between types in [Five Types of Event Venues in Tokyo](https://www.conec-tando.com/en/column/five-types-of-event-venues-in-tokyo/).

To check venue availability and locations, see [bizintokyo](https://bizintokyo.com/en/).

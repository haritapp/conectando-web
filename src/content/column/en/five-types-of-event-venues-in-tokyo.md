In the [previous article](https://www.conec-tando.com/en/column/what-to-decide-before-choosing-an-event-venue-in-tokyo/), we said: decide your date, headcount, budget and purpose before you look at venues. Once those are set, the next question is usually "OK, so what kind of venue?" Tokyo's venues fall into roughly five types. But let's say the conclusion up front: our advice is not to choose by type.

## 1. Hotels

Many Tokyo hotels have not just banquet rooms but full-size conference halls, and large corporate events are often held there. The strength is that catering and accommodation are all in one place. If you want prestige and a strong brand impression, a hotel is the natural choice — but it will cost considerably more.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/hotel_newotani/

## 2. Large convention centers

[Tokyo Big Sight](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/tokyo_big_sight/), [Makuhari Messe](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/makuhari/), [Tokyo International Forum](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/international_forum/) and others. These are for exhibitions and conferences with over 1,000 attendees. Two things to watch. First, location: Makuhari Messe and [Pacifico Yokohama](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/pacifico_yokohama/) are much farther from central Tokyo than they look on a map to someone who doesn't live here. Second, scale: be honest about whether that many people will really come. A large hall that is half empty looks very, very empty.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/international_forum/

## 3. Rental halls

Bellesalle, [Shibuya Hikarie](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/hikarie/) (around 1,000 people) and similar. They sit in central Tokyo, close to stations, and in practice this is where a large share of Tokyo events actually happen. The venue is essentially a box: catering, sound and staging are arranged by you.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/hikarie/

## 4. Unique venues

Museums, gardens, historic buildings, wedding halls on weekdays. This is the option when you want an event people remember. Because renting for events is not their main business, they are often cheaper than you would expect. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government runs [TOKYO Unique Venues](https://uniquevenues-en.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/), a list of 70-plus facilities with price guidelines, and Japanese-language sites like [Spacemarket](https://www.spacemarket.com/) let you browse many more. The trade-off is that each venue has its own rules and more things to confirm.

## 5. Small and mid-size spaces

Meeting-room chains like TKP, rented-out restaurants, coworking spaces, and public facilities such as [Tokyo Innovation Base (TIB)](https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/tokyo-innovation-base/). These are a very good way to start small. Free or very cheap venues tend to have limited availability and conditions attached, so check early.

https://bizintokyo.com/en/venues/tokyo-innovation-base/

## Don't start from the type

When organizers start with "let's do a hotel" or "let's use a convention center," the choice often ends up clashing with the budget or the realistic turnout. In Tokyo, the approach with the fewest surprises is to fix your purpose and budget first, then look at venues across all five types without deciding the type in advance.

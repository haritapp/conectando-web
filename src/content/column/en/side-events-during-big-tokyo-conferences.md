When you hold an event in Tokyo, one option is to run it as a side event during a big conference. It is often easier to attract people than holding it on its own.

## Why time it with a big event

There are two reasons.

First, speakers are easier to get. During a big event, speakers come to Tokyo for that event anyway.

Second, attendees are easier to get. Many big events introduce side events on their official website. During that week, Tokyo is full of people who love startups.

We did this ourselves. In May 2025, we held "New York Silicon Alley Meetup #12" as a side event of SusHi Tech Tokyo 2025. The event introduced the New York startup scene in Tokyo, and 131 people came. ([See the case study](/en/case/detail/newyorksiliconalley12/))

## Big events you can hold a side event around

- **[SusHi Tech Tokyo](https://sushitech-startup.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/en/related-events)**: Organized by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and held every spring at Tokyo Big Sight (April 27–29 in 2026). It invites "partner events" held during the conference period.
- **[Japan Fintech Week](https://www.fsa.go.jp/policy/japanfintechweek/2027/index_en.html)**: A fintech week organized by Japan's Financial Services Agency and others. In 2027 it runs from February 22 to March 5. It invites "partner events" held during the week.
- **[Tokyo Tech Week](https://tech-week.jp/)**: Held December 9–11 in 2026. Registration is free, and each host can submit up to three events.

Dates and conditions are as of November 2026. Always check the official website before you apply.

## When a side event is not a good fit

Timing your event with a big conference does not guarantee that people will come.

**1. The big event does not match your theme**

Even a very big event is not much use if its theme does not match yours. For example, holding a startup event during Tokyo Game Show makes little sense, and the reverse is also true. Choose which big event to join with a cool head.

**2. You have no Japanese partner**

Without a Japanese partner, attracting people may be difficult. It helps to run the event together with a Japanese company in the same industry, a local community, or a co-hosting organization. For example, if your event is about AI, it is better to hold it together with a Japanese AI community.

## What to watch out for

**1. Venues are surprisingly hard to book**

During a big event, other organizers are also looking for venues nearby.

**2. Read the official listing process carefully**

For some events, if you do not contact them early, your event will not be featured on the official website.

**3. Apply early. Don't wait until all your speakers are confirmed**

Early applications are more likely to be accepted and promoted. Applying early works better than waiting until every speaker is confirmed.

**4. Check the rules carefully**

Each big event has its own rules for side events. Events that are too sales-focused may not be accepted. For example, at Tokyo Tech Week 2026, at least one person responsible for the event must take a training course, and panels with two or more speakers should not be all-male.
